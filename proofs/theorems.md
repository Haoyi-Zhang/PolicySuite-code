# Semantic supports and exact request suites

## Scope and observation

All statements concern explicitly finite request sets Q and flat rule lists, not a production policy service. A rule has Permit/Deny effect and false/true/error activation on each request. First-applicable (FA) returns the first non-NotApplicable rule result, including an error. The internal decisions are N, P, D, IP, ID, and IDP. A false rule returns N, a true rule returns its effect, and an erroneous rule returns IP or ID according to its effect. Deny-overrides (DO) evaluates these clauses in order: any D gives D; any IDP, or an ID together with IP or P, gives IDP; a remaining ID gives ID; a remaining P gives P; a remaining IP gives IP; otherwise return N. Permit-overrides (PO) exchanges P and D everywhere, including their indeterminate types. Public observations identify IP, ID, and IDP as I only at the policy root. Ordered overrides have the same observed results in this model. An individual mutation changes one rule effect, deletes one rule, or performs another explicitly declared operation. Each obligation retains its snapshot identity.

For mutation m of snapshot s, D_m is the set of requests on which the public result of the mutant differs from that snapshot's unmutated result. An empty D_m is equivalent on Q; it is listed, not counted as detectable. A suite is adequate exactly when it intersects every nonempty D_m. Cardinality counts unique requests; replay on every snapshot is assumed. The asymptotic statements range over finite inputs of unbounded size. Executable safety caps are implementation limits, not constants in the complexity theorems.

## 1. Exact finite reduction

Two requests with identical activation values for every original rule of every snapshot have identical public observations before and after every declared flip, deletion, constant true/false activation replacement, adjacent swap, and root-combiner replacement. Each operation changes the same position or combiner and introduces no new request-dependent predicate. Thus the complete result vectors coincide. Choosing one representative per joint activation class preserves all hitting relations and the minimum. A partition based only on old rules is not sufficient for a revision that introduces a new activation boundary. The implementation constructs full finite supports before quotienting; this is not a symbolic enumeration-free algorithm.

Every nonempty finite support contains an inclusion-minimal nonempty support of its own snapshot. Removing empty supports, duplicate supports, and strict supersets therefore preserves adequate suites. This elimination is classical and is not a novel set-cover algorithm.

## 2. Masking lemma for error-free overrides

In a DO snapshot, let B be the union of all deny-rule scopes. For a permit rule r with scope T_r, its flip support is F_r=T_r\B. Its deletion support is U_r=T_r\(B union the scopes of all other permit rules). For a deny rule d, both flip and deletion support equal V_d=T_d\(the union of all other deny-rule scopes).

Proof: A new deny caused by flipping r can change an error-free result only where r applies and no deny already applies; there it changes Permit to Deny. Deleting r changes a result only where r is the sole applicable permit and no deny applies. Removing or flipping d changes Deny exactly where no other deny applies. In that region deletion returns Permit or NotApplicable, and flipping returns Permit, each distinct from Deny. These exhaust the possible outcomes.

The V_d are pairwise disjoint and disjoint from every F_r. Each U_r is a subset of F_r and is disjoint from every F_j for j unequal to r. If the nonempty F_r form a laminar family, all nonempty local flip/deletion supports form a laminar family. In particular their inclusion-minimal distinct members are pairwise disjoint. The corresponding PO result follows by exchanging Permit with Deny everywhere, including the dominant and subordinate rule roles. No restriction on overlaps among the original dominant-effect scopes is necessary. Raw subordinate laminarity is sufficient because removing the common set B preserves inclusion and disjointness, but the masked condition can be weaker.

## 3. Error-free first-applicable lemma

Let T_i be rule i's true scope and V_i=T_i\(union of all earlier true scopes). An effect flip is observable exactly on V_i. Deletion is observable on a subset E_i of V_i: after deleting i, the later result may equal or differ from its effect. Every V_i is disjoint from every other V_j. Consequently each rule supplies a two-element nested family E_i subset V_i, and all inclusion-minimal nonempty supports are disjoint. No laminarity restriction on the original T_i is needed.

Proof: Earlier applicable rules mask any change at i. On V_i, the original result is the rule's P/D effect, and flipping changes it. Deletion can affect only the same visibility region. A request has at most one first applicable rule, proving disjointness.

## 4. Two-snapshot minimum and packing certificate

Let A_1,...,A_a and B_1,...,B_b be the distinct inclusion-minimal nonempty supports in the two snapshots. Suppose the A sets are pairwise disjoint, as are the B sets. Form a bipartite graph whose edge ij exists iff A_i intersects B_j. If nu is the maximum matching size, the minimum suite cardinality is a+b-nu. An equal-sized collection of pairwise disjoint actual mutation supports is a lower-bound certificate.

Upper bound: Choose one request from each matched intersection, then one request in each unmatched vertex. This hits all minimal supports. Requests from different matched edges cannot coincide because the supports within each layer are disjoint. An unmatched vertex cannot share its chosen point with a matched intersection, since that would duplicate a support within a layer. Two unmatched vertices in opposite layers cannot intersect: their edge could be added to the matching. Thus these are a+b-nu distinct requests.

Lower bound and constructive certificate: Starting from all unmatched left vertices, traverse unmatched edges left-to-right and matched edges right-to-left. Let Z_A and Z_B be the reached vertices. No unmatched right vertex is reached, because the path from a free left vertex to it would augment the matching. Every reached right vertex has a reached matched left partner, and every reached matched left vertex has its right partner reached. The set C=(A\Z_A) union Z_B covers all graph edges. Otherwise an uncovered edge would run from a reached left vertex to an unreached right vertex; an unmatched edge would have been traversed, and a matched edge's partner is reached. Exactly one endpoint of each matched edge belongs to C; no unmatched vertex belongs to C. Hence |C|=nu. Its complement W=Z_A union (B\Z_B) has a+b-nu vertices and no cross-layer edge. Combined with within-layer disjointness, their actual supports are pairwise disjoint. Any adequate suite needs one distinct request per support in W. This matches the upper bound.

Maximum matching can be obtained by repeated augmentations. If a matching were not maximum, its symmetric difference with a larger one would contain an alternating path with one more edge from the larger matching and free endpoints, hence an augmenting path. Terminating with no such path is therefore sufficient. This is a classical matching argument, used here rather than claimed as a new graph theorem.

The empty-obligation case has a=b=nu=0. The sufficient policy lemmas are not necessary: the finite support test can admit additional policies or mutation profiles.

## 5. Public errors: one FA snapshot and a sharp two-snapshot packing gap

For arbitrary ternary activation, define V_i as the requests whose first non-false original rule is i. These regions are disjoint. The local flip support F_i consists of the true-activation requests in V_i: flipping an error changes its internal effect type but not the public I. The deletion support E_i is an arbitrary subset of V_i. No local edit of i changes requests outside V_i.

For one snapshot, each disjoint region has at most two nonempty obligations. Its exact cost is zero if neither is nonempty; one if exactly one is nonempty or both intersect; and two if both are nonempty and disjoint. A common point realizes the intersecting case; otherwise choose one point per nonempty support. A packing of equal size chooses one support in a one-cost group and both supports in a two-cost group. Groups are disjoint. Thus one FA snapshot with public errors still has an optimum-sized disjoint-support packing, even though its minimal supports need not be pairwise disjoint.

For two such snapshots, let k_0,k_1 be their individual optima and let lambda be the maximum number of pairwise disjoint nonempty supports in their union. Each individual optimum equals a packing, so max(k_0,k_1) <= lambda. The union of two individually optimal suites is adequate. Therefore lambda <= OPT <= k_0+k_1 <= 2 max(k_0,k_1) <= 2 lambda.

The factor 2 is sharp. Take Q={a,b,c}. Snapshot A is FA with ordered rules P:(true at a,b; error at c), D:(true at b), P:(true at a,b,c). Its only nonempty distinct local supports are the first rule's flip {a,b} and deletion {b,c}. Later rules are originally masked. Snapshot B has one P rule true at a,c, whose flip and deletion support is {a,c}. The three pairwise intersecting edges of a triangle require two requests and admit a packing of only one support. All original true scopes in each snapshot are laminar. k disjoint copies have OPT=2k and lambda=k. The fractional hitting-set optimum per triangle is 3/2: assigning 1/2 to each request is feasible, and summing the three constraints proves the lower bound. This invalidates tight packing under public errors, not polynomial-time solvability of every two-snapshot error case; the latter is not settled here.

## 6. Three snapshots and conflict-graph limitations

Given perfect 3-dimensional matching on X x Y x Z, with each part of size n and allowed triples E, make each triple a request. In snapshot X, put one permit rule per element x with scope the triples containing x, and similarly for Y and Z. Each snapshot's scopes form a partition. Under DO, PO or FA, each rule's local flip and deletion supports are precisely its nonempty block. A suite of at most n requests covers every block in all three snapshots iff it is a perfect 3-dimensional matching: each request covers exactly one element of each part; n requests covering n elements in a part cannot repeat any. The converse is immediate. If an input element has no triple, the matching input is negative; map it to the fixed negative parity instance described below instead of silently dropping an empty support. This is a polynomial reduction. Membership in NP follows by evaluating every proposed request against the explicit supports. Thus three-snapshot minimum selection is NP-complete in decision form, even for error-free partition scopes and the local profile.

Parity instance: Q={000,011,101,110}; each snapshot corresponds to one bit coordinate, with two permit scopes for zero and one. Every two snapshots admit a two-request suite. All three require three: every pair of distinct parity requests agrees on a coordinate and misses that coordinate's other value. Any three cover all six blocks. A maximum disjoint-support packing has size two; blocks from different coordinate layers intersect. Assigning weight 1/2 to all four requests gives fractional cost two, also bounded below by either layer.

A graph placing an edge between opposing-effect rules that can co-activate is edgeless in every base policy above. It remains edgeless within each single-mutant snapshot: a flipped rule's scope is still disjoint from every other scope. Hence bounded treewidth of these per-snapshot conflict graphs alone cannot imply an FPT exact algorithm across arbitrary histories unless P=NP. This statement does not apply to a graph containing cross-snapshot obligation/request incidence.

Even one unstructured snapshot is hard if only the base conflict graph is used. For any nonempty hitting-set scopes T_i, duplicate each scope as two permit rules under DO. All deletions are equivalent; each flip has support T_i. Thus the minimum suite is the original hitting-set optimum despite the base graph having no opposing-effect edges. Mutant-aware conflicts may be present in this second reduction, so its claim is explicitly base-graph-only.

## 7. Output-preserving revisions need not preserve adequacy

Let Q be partitioned into n>=2 nonempty blocks. The old snapshot has n identical universal permit rules. The new snapshot has n permit rules, one per block. Use DO or FA consistently. Both return Permit everywhere. In the old snapshot, all detectable local supports equal Q (under FA only the first flip is detectable); hence its minimum is one. In the new snapshot every block occurs as a support, so its minimum is n. The common minimum is n. Thus a revision with no changed public output can increase the mutation-adequate minimum from one to n. For the all-permit construction under PO, the old minimum is zero instead: another universal permit masks every deletion and every effect flip. The new and common minima still equal n. Thus the retained campaign has 13 one-to-n DO/FA cases and seven zero-to-n PO cases, not twenty one-to-n cases. This is a separation of objectives, not a defect claim about change-impact techniques designed to preserve different obligations.

## 8. General finite certificate and failure witness

Let C(q) be the obligations detected by q and U a subset of nonempty obligations. Define f(empty)=0. For any pivot m in U, f(U)=1+min over q in D_m of f(U\C(q)). The child is a strict subset because q detects m. Any adequate suite for U has a request q detecting m and its remaining requests cover the child; conversely q together with a child cover covers U. Induction on |U| proves the recurrence.

A complete finite DAG records each visited U, f(U), and pivot, including all distinct children of that pivot. The checker recomputes every support and child from the original input, verifies the zero state, all exact recurrences, and equality of the root value with the cardinality of a verified covering suite. Its acceptance therefore proves finite optimality under its stated trusted semantics. A DAG can be exponential; no compact general lower-bound certificate is claimed.

For a suite that misses a nonempty support, scan mutations in their declared order, select the first missed mutation, and select the least request index in its support. Returning that concrete request and the two unequal public results is a canonical uncovered witness under these orders. It is not an automatically inferred production counterexample or proof of policy intent. Empty supports cannot have such a witness; their equivalence is only over the declared Q.

## Validation and limits

Written arguments above are mathematical proofs, not proof-assistant mechanizations. The repository contains separate finite validation of the masking/visibility premises, all pairs of four-request disjoint support families, full tiny policy optimization, the public-error packing construction, and the stated parity/triangle controls. A separate checker implementation reduces common-code dependence but is not an independent external audit or native XACML conformance oracle. Closest-work full-text clearance and public-policy/native-language validation remain explicit limitations.
